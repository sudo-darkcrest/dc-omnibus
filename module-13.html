Wireless, Cellular and Keeping Networks Healthy

Why this matters. Wi-Fi is how most users connect, and it is the most common source of "the network is slow". Running a network also means monitoring it, planning for failure and fixing problems methodically. Network+ Domains 2, 3 and 5 meet here, with overlap into A+ (wireless, tools) and Security+ (wireless attacks). The module ends with a 60-question Network+ readiness exam covering Modules 9 to 13.

You will be able to

  • Explain RF (Radio Frequency) basics: channels, width, interference, RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator), SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio), antennas, MIMO (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output), beamforming and OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiple Access).
  • Compare Wi-Fi 4 to Wi-Fi 7, plan non-overlapping channels and choose WPA2/WPA3 (WPA2: Wi-Fi Protected Access 2; WPA3: Wi-Fi Protected Access 3) Personal or Enterprise.
  • Describe cellular (4G/5G, private cellular), Bluetooth, NFC (Near Field Communication), RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) and satellite.
  • Use monitoring data and metrics (latency, jitter, loss, uptime), and DR (Disaster Recovery) terms (RPO (Recovery Point Objective), RTO (Recovery Time Objective), MTTR (Mean Time To Repair), MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)).
  • Pick the right command or tool and follow the troubleshooting method to a fix.

1. Radio basics

Wi-Fi is radio: electromagnetic waves at 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz. Higher frequency means a shorter wavelength (2.4 GHz ≈ 12.5 cm, 5 GHz ≈ 6 cm, 6 GHz ≈ 5 cm), more available channels and speed, but less range and worse penetration through walls.

TermMeaning
Channel / channel widthA slice of spectrum. Wider channels (20, 40, 80, 160, 320 MHz) carry more data but leave fewer non-overlapping channels.
InterferenceCo-channel: APs (Access Point) on the same channel take turns (contention). Adjacent-channel: partially overlapping channels corrupt each other (worse). Non-Wi-Fi sources: microwaves, Bluetooth, cordless phones (2.4 GHz).
Reflection, refraction, absorption, scatteringMetal reflects; water, people and concrete absorb; signal weakens with distance (free-space path loss).
RSSIReceived signal strength, in dBm. −30 excellent, −67 good for voice and video, −70 OK for data, −80 unusable.
SNRSignal minus noise floor (dB). 25 dB+ is good; below 15 dB is poor.
AntennasOmnidirectional radiates in all horizontal directions (ceiling APs). Directional (Yagi, patch, parabolic) focuses energy for long hallways or point-to-point bridges. Gain is measured in dBi.
MIMO / MU-MIMO (Multi-User Multiple-Input Multiple-Output)Multiple antennas send several spatial streams at once; MU-MIMO serves several clients simultaneously.
BeamformingSteers signal toward a client for a stronger link.
OFDMAWi-Fi 6+ splits a channel into resource units so many clients transmit in one burst (great for dense areas).

3. Wireless security

ProtocolEncryptionStatus
WEP (Wired Equivalent Privacy)RC4 with weak IVs (Initialization Vector)Broken: cracked in minutes. Never use.
WPA (Wi-Fi Protected Access)TKIP (Temporal Key Integrity Protocol; RC4-based)Deprecated.
WPA2AES-CCMPStill common. Personal (PSK) uses a shared passphrase; Enterprise uses 802.1X with a RADIUS (Remote Authentication Dial-In User Service) server and per-user credentials or certificates (EAP-TLS, PEAP (Protected Extensible Authentication Protocol)).
WPA3AES (Advanced Encryption Standard; GCMP-256 (Galois/Counter Mode Protocol) in 192-bit Enterprise)Current. Personal uses SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals), which resists offline passphrase guessing and gives forward secrecy. Enhanced Open (OWE) encrypts open networks.
  • Captive portal: a web page users must accept or log into before access (hotels, guests).
  • Rogue AP: an unauthorized AP plugged into the network. Evil twin: an attacker's AP mimicking a real SSID (Service Set Identifier) to capture logins. Defend with WIDS/WIPS (WIDS: Wireless Intrusion Detection System; WIPS: Wireless Intrusion Prevention System), 802.1X, certificate validation and user training.
  • Disable WPS (Wi-Fi Protected Setup), change default admin credentials, use separate guest SSIDs/VLANs (VLAN: Virtual Local Area Network), and keep AP firmware patched. Hiding the SSID and MAC (Media Access Control) filtering are weak controls, not security.

4. Site surveys, placement and roaming

A site survey measures RF coverage and interference before (predictive) and after (validation) deployment. The output is a heat map of signal strength. Good design: plan for capacity, not just coverage; overlap cells by about 15 to 20% at −67 dBm so clients roam smoothly; use 1/6/11 on 2.4 GHz; lower transmit power in dense areas; mount APs on ceilings away from metal. Roaming is a client moving between APs on the same SSID; 802.11r (fast transition), 802.11k (neighbor reports) and 802.11v (BSS transition) make it faster. A wireless LAN (Local Area Network) controller centrally manages AP channels, power and roaming.

6. Network operations: monitoring, availability and recovery

Monitoring sources

  • SNMP (Simple Network Management Protocol) polls and traps for interface counters, CPU (Central Processing Unit), temperature.
  • Syslog events from every device into a central server.
  • SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) correlates logs for security events and alerts.
  • Flow data (NetFlow/IPFIX/sFlow) for top talkers and anomalies.
  • Packet capture for deep troubleshooting; API (Application Programming Interface)/streaming telemetry on modern gear.

Performance metrics

  • Bandwidth (capacity) vs throughput (actual) vs goodput (useful data).
  • Latency: delay, usually round-trip in ms.
  • Jitter: variation in latency.
  • Packet loss: % of packets that never arrive.
  • Uptime / availability: 99.9% ≈ 8.8 h downtime per year; 99.99% ≈ 53 min; 99.999% ≈ 5.3 min.
  • Interface errors: CRC (Cyclic Redundancy Check) errors (cabling), runts/giants, discards (congestion).
TermMeaningExample
RPORecovery Point Objective: maximum acceptable data loss, measured in timeRPO 15 min → back up or replicate at least every 15 min
RTORecovery Time Objective: maximum acceptable downtimeRTO 4 h → service back within 4 hours
MTTRMean Time To Repair/RecoverAverage 2 h to replace a failed switch
MTBFMean Time Between Failures (repairable items)Power supply MTBF 100,000 h

High availability: redundancy at every layer (dual power supplies, NIC (Network Interface Card) teaming, LACP (Link Aggregation Control Protocol), redundant switches and routers with FHRP (First Hop Redundancy Protocol), multiple ISPs (Internet Service Provider)). Active-active uses both paths at once; active-passive keeps one on standby. DR sites: hot (running, minutes), warm (hardware ready, hours to days), cold (space and power only, days to weeks). Test DR plans with tabletop exercises and failover tests.

7. Network troubleshooting method and tools

  1. Identify the problem (gather information, question users, identify symptoms, determine what changed, duplicate the problem, approach multiple problems individually).
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause (question the obvious; consider top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top OSI (Open Systems Interconnection), or divide and conquer).
  3. Test the theory to determine the cause (if not confirmed, establish a new theory or escalate).
  4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and identify potential effects.
  5. Implement the solution or escalate as necessary.
  6. Verify full system functionality and implement preventive measures.
  7. Document findings, actions, outcomes and lessons learned.

Memory aids

2.4 GHz non-overlapping channels: 1, 6, 11: "one, six, eleven: Wi-Fi heaven".
Wi-Fi numbers: 4 = n, 5 = ac, 6 = ax, 7 = be: "Never Act Axious, Be calm".
RPO = Point in time you can roll back to (data loss). RTO = Time to get back up (downtime).
Troubleshooting: "I Think That Everyone Is Very Determined": Identify, Theory, Test, Establish plan, Implement, Verify, Document.

Exam trapWhy it is wrong
"Hide the SSID to secure Wi-Fi."Hidden SSIDs are visible in probe traffic; use WPA3/802.1X.
"Use channels 1, 2 and 3 to avoid interference."Adjacent channels overlap; 1/6/11 don't.
"Raise every AP to full power for better coverage."Causes co-channel contention and sticky clients; tune power to the design.
"MTBF measures repair time."MTBF is time between failures; MTTR is repair time.
"Jump straight to replacing hardware."Follow the method: identify, theorize, test first.
