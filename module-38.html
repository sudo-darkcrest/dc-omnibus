Study Hub: Plans, Crosswalk and Practice Exams

Why this matters. Knowing the material is only half of passing. You also need a plan, a clear view of what each exam weighs most, honest practice under time pressure and a calm exam day. This module turns the whole Omnibus into a study system: it maps every exam domain to the modules that teach it, builds a week-by-week plan from your exam date, and gives you timed practice exams and one combined flashcard deck.

  • Use proven study methods (active recall, spacing, interleaving) and exam-day tactics.

CompTIA and ISACA (Information Systems Audit and Control Association) report scaled scores. A scaled score is not a percentage: 750 out of 900 on Security+ does not mean you need 83% correct. Aim for 85% or more on practice exams to give yourself a buffer.

2. How to study so it sticks

Research on learning points to three habits that work far better than rereading notes:

Active recall

Test yourself before you feel ready. Every quiz and flashcard in this Omnibus is active recall.

Spaced repetition

Review just before you would forget. The Leitner boxes schedule this for you: box 1 every session, box 5 every two weeks.

Interleaving

Mix topics in one session. Practice exams do this, which trains you to tell similar ideas apart (RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks) 5 vs 6, IDS (Intrusion Detection System) vs IPS (Intrusion Prevention System)).

Memory is like a path through a field. Walk it once and the grass grows back in days. Walk it again every few days and it becomes a trail that lasts.

Where to spend your next hour

Multiply a domain's weight by how weak you are in it. A 24% domain where you score 55% is a much better use of time than a 14% domain where you score 85%. The practice exam engine shows your score by domain and links each weak area to the module that teaches it.

3. Exam-day tips

Performance-based questions (PBQs)

  • CompTIA exams often put PBQs at the start. They can be slow.
  • Read the task, do what you can quickly, flag it and move on. Come back with the time you have left (verify that your exam lets you return).
  • Partial credit is possible on some PBQs, so never leave one empty.
  • Every lab in this Omnibus practises the drag, match and configure skills PBQs use.

Time management

  • Divide the time by the questions: 90 min for 90 questions is about one minute each; 240 min for 150 is about 1.6 minutes.
  • Halfway check: at half the time, be at least half way through.
  • Leave 10 to 15 minutes to review flagged questions.

Flagging

  • If you're stuck after reading twice, choose your best guess, flag it and move on.
  • A later question sometimes reminds you of the answer.
  • Don't change answers without a clear reason. Your first reasoned choice is often right.

BEST, FIRST, MOST and NOT

  • Underline the qualifier in your head. Several options may be true; only one fits.
  • FIRST means order: map the options onto the process (troubleshooting steps, incident response).
  • BEST on CISM (Certified Information Security Manager) and CISA means the manager or auditor view: business risk and governance first.
  • NOT, EXCEPT and LEAST flip the question.

Read every option

  • Don't stop at the first answer that looks right. A better one may follow.
  • Eliminate clearly wrong options to turn a 1-in-4 guess into a 1-in-2.
  • Distrust absolutes ("always", "never") unless the topic really is absolute.
  • For long scenarios, read the question at the end first.

Test center vs online proctoring

  • Test center: fewer tech risks, lockers for belongings, a provided computer. Arrive 15 to 30 minutes early with your ID (Identification).
  • Online (e.g., Pearson VUE OnVUE, PSI (PSI Services, a test delivery company)): run the system test on the same computer and network the day before, clear your desk, use a quiet room, keep your phone out of reach, and have your ID ready for the room scan.
  • Breaks and rules differ by vendor (verify before the day).
Don't book an exam whose code differs from what you studied. Check the exam code on your booking against the objectives you used (for example SY0-701, N10-009, 220-1201/1202, and ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library) 4 vs ITIL Version 5 Foundation).
The night before: sleep, don't cram. On the day: eat, arrive early, and if you feel anxious, take three slow breaths before you start. One hard question means nothing; the next one may be easy.

5. A sample study week

Consistency beats cramming. Here is a realistic week for someone working full time and studying for their first certification, about 7 hours in total.

DayTimeActivity
Monday45 minRead one lesson section; make notes in your own words
Tuesday45 minDo the module's labs; write a five-sentence summary of each
Wednesday30 minFlashcards (Leitner review), then read the next section
Thursday45 minModule knowledge check; read every explanation, including for questions you got right
Friday15 minFlashcards only
Saturday2 hoursHands-on practice in a home lab or simulator, plus one practice exam set
SundayRestRest matters: memory consolidates during sleep

Book the exam once your practice scores are consistently about 85% or higher across every domain. A booked date is a powerful motivator, but booking too early adds stress.

How the new modules fit together

  • Networking: CCNA (Module 14) leads into CCNP Security and the CCIE (Module 15).
  • Security: Security+ (Modules 16 to 18) and ethical hacking (Module 19) lead into CISSP and CCSP (Module 20).
  • Cloud: virtualization and Nutanix (Module 24); AWS fundamentals then associate and specialty (25 and 26); Azure fundamentals then administrator (27 and 28); Google Cloud (29).
  • Governance: privacy (Module 33) and risk and IT governance (Module 34) sit alongside CISM and CISA (35 and 36).
Most of the top-paying certifications need three to five years of experience. A common route is A+ or Network+, then Security+ or CCNA, then a cloud fundamentals exam, then one of the advanced certifications above in your chosen area.
